ΕΠΟΥ ΘΕΩΙ · follow the signal

The loop

Five stages, each feeding the next. The last one closes the circle: the system's measured record changes how it forecasts tomorrow.

I

Ingest

48 feeds fetched concurrently — global wires, FT and The Economist, conflict-analysis shops, government primary sources (UN, travel advisories, the Pentagon, IAEA), Gulf and regional press, Bluesky voices, plus early-warning queries for mobilization, embassy evacuations and coups. Alongside them, GDELT — machine-coded coverage across 100+ languages — supplies escalation ratios per watch topic: how loud the world has become about Iran or Taiwan this week, measured against its own 12-day baseline. Every scan records its exact reading list, so any forecast can be traced to the headlines behind it.

II

Forecast

The oracle turns a source-balanced batch into concrete, falsifiable statements with probabilities, deadlines and the world region they bear on. The output schema forces it to write its reasoning before committing the number — base rate first, probability last. A statement that recites history, restates an open claim, or contradicts one is refused in code, not merely discouraged in the prompt.

III

Resolve

When deadlines pass, the oracle re-reads the news and proposes outcomes with cited evidence. A human confirms every resolution — the ledger's integrity is never delegated to the model that is being graded.

IV

Update

New evidence moves open forecasts the way a real forecaster's credence moves — revisions are automatic, cited, bounded by guards against drift and hindsight, and kept in a full audit history on every card.

V

Learn

Once 30 forecasts have resolved, Pythia's measured miscalibration — "in the 70–80% range you said 75%, but 20% happened" — is fed back into its own forecasting prompt. It corrects itself from evidence, not vibes.

Three ways it predicts

Every forecast declares its method, and each method is scored separately — so the ledger reveals not just whether the system forecasts well, but which kind of thinking earns its keep.

◆ event

Will it happen?

The news says something may happen. Pythia states the odds that it actually does.

→ consequence

What follows?

Reference-class forecasting: the closest past episodes, their base rate, and the adjustment for today.

ΓΝΩΘΙ ΣΑΥΤΟΝ · know thyself

The science that keeps it honest

Most AI demos ask to be believed. Pythia asks to be checked — the design borrows its discipline from forecasting research, not from chatbots.

  • Reason before numberUnder constrained decoding, models emit fields in schema order — so the schema puts base rate and causal chain before the probability, making the number condition on the reasoning instead of justifying it afterwards.
  • Time-weighted scoringA resolved forecast is scored on its average credence across its whole open life. A last-minute jump to 95% counts only for the sliver of time it was held — the ledger measures sustained judgment, and cannot be gamed.
  • The human gateThe oracle proposes; you resolve. Absence from a small news batch is never treated as proof, post-horizon revisions are refused as hindsight, and every "happened" must cite its articles.
  • Noise never becomes instructionCalibration feedback reaches the prompt only past 30 resolutions, and only for biases exceeding twice their binomial standard error. Below that, silence.
  • Every claim carries its evidenceForecasts link their motivating articles, proposals cite their proof, revisions keep their history, and each scan's full reading digest is one click away.
  • On-premise by defaultThe live oracle is a local open model via Ollama — nothing leaves the machine. Set ANTHROPIC_API_KEY and it switches to Claude Opus 5 in seconds, and the ledger will say which one forecasts better.

ΜΗΔΕΝ ΑΓΑΝ · nothing in excess

Built like it handles money

7adversarial multi-agent review rounds
148reviewer & verifier agents deployed
97confirmed findings — every one fixed
23plausible findings refuted before wasting a fix

Every feature passed the same gauntlet: specialist reviewers attacked the design — correctness, security, forecasting methodology, UX — and a second wave of agents tried to refute each finding before anything was changed. The survivors included genuinely deep catches: a schema field-order that quietly defeated base-rate anchoring, and a scoring rule that would have let the system flatter its own track record. Each fix is pinned by tests, validated live against a real 22B model on real news, and committed to version control.
